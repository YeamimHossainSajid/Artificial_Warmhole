\section*{Introduction}
An artificial wormhole is a theoretically constructed or simulated tunnel through spacetime that connects two distant points, functioning as a cosmic shortcut. Grounded in the mathematics of Albert Einstein and Nathan Rosen (often called an Einstein-Rosen bridge), these structures are currently explored not for transporting spaceships, but for teleporting quantum information.

\section*{The Mechanics of Teleportation via Wormholes}
The concept of teleporting through a wormhole bridges two major pillars of physics: general relativity (gravity and spacetime) and quantum mechanics. This connection is driven by a theoretical framework known as \textbf{ER=EPR}.

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{ER} stands for Einstein-Rosen (wormholes).
    \item \textbf{EPR} stands for Einstein-Podolsky-Rosen (quantum entanglement).
\end{itemize}

The ER=EPR conjecture suggests that two entangled quantum particles are actually connected by a microscopic wormhole. When teleportation occurs through an artificial wormhole, it is not physical matter moving through a pipe; it is information transferring through entanglement.

\subsection*{1. Establishing the Connection (Entanglement)}
To make teleportation possible, you first need two quantum systems that are deeply entangled---meaning the state of one system instantly correlates with the state of the other, no matter the distance. In the context of a wormhole, these two entangled systems act as the two ``mouths'' of the bridge.

\subsection*{2. Opening the Wormhole (Negative Energy)}
A standard theoretical wormhole naturally pinches off and collapses before anything can cross it. To make it ``traversable,'' physicists must introduce a shockwave of negative energy (or exotic matter). This negative energy pushes outward against gravity, propping the throat of the wormhole open just long enough for something to pass through.

\subsection*{3. Scrambling and Unscrambling (The Teleportation)}
When a unit of quantum information (a qubit) is sent into one side of the wormhole:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item The information is rapidly scrambled and absorbed by the first entangled system.
    \item The negative energy shockwave allows this scrambled information to bypass the collapse of the space.
    \item The information emerges at the second entangled system (the other mouth), where it is unscrambled and reassembled into its original state.
\end{enumerate}

The qubit has been teleported. It did not physically fly across the room---it traversed the entanglement bridge.

\section*{Real-World Simulation: The Quantum Computer Experiment}
In late 2022, researchers used Google's Sycamore quantum processor to create the first simulated ``artificial wormhole.'' They did not tear a hole in physical space; instead, they created a highly complex quantum circuit that mathematically behaved exactly like a traversable wormhole. 

They inserted a qubit into one side of the entangled circuit, applied a simulated pulse of negative energy, and watched the qubit emerge intact on the other side. The information traveled between the two systems through a mechanism that perfectly mirrored the gravitational physics of a wormhole, proving that quantum teleportation and wormhole traversal are mathematically equivalent.
